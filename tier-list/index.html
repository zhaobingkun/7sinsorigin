7DS Origin Character Investment Guide

Choose your next upgrade by the problem it can solve in your owned roster, the cost to reach a usable setup, and what that spending delays. Start with one encounter you cannot finish reliably.

Who should receive the next upgrade?

Original editorial analysis: the following decisions are a budgeting method, not official character ratings. “Build” applies to a particular account and task; it does not make a hero universally stronger.

  1. Define a result. Write the encounter, difficulty and solo or party context, then the failure: dying before an opening, surviving until time runs out, or failing to activate a required skill sequence. “More power” is too vague to evaluate.
  2. Identify an owned candidate. Record the exact hero, equipped weapon, current skill text and relevant unlocks. Describe the job you need: sustained damage, survival, or enabling another hero's condition. These are worksheet roles, not a claim that every hero supplies them.
  3. Choose one decision. Build toward one displayed upgrade if you can explain how it addresses the failure. Trial with existing resources if activation or compatibility is uncertain. Defer if a required partner is missing, the cost is unknown, or the encounter already clears reliably.

If the problem is a missed input or an unavailable skill, first follow the team troubleshooting workflow. Buying more stats before identifying the failure can leave the original problem intact.

What can official skill descriptions actually establish?

The Lancelot official-details summary links Netmarble's weapon-specific skills. The official release notice says later patches may change them and directs readers to in-game descriptions for the most accurate details. It describes different Staff, Longsword and Gauntlets kits; a character name alone is insufficient to evaluate the setup.

Source-grounded example, not a recommendation: the published Longsword passive depends on a successful perfectly charged Special Attack. Before investing for that mechanic, check whether you can perform it against your target with your controls. Write down successful activations and why attempts fail. That checks a prerequisite; it does not establish Lancelot's rank or compare his damage with another hero.

The Elaine Pick Up notice answers acquisition questions. Its draw rules do not establish a combat role, a Lancelot partnership, or a reason to replace an owned hero. Treat “can I obtain this hero?” and “does this setup solve my problem?” as separate decisions.

What does one usable upgrade cost?

Compare the remaining cost from your account today, not the total already spent. Past spending is not evidence that the next upgrade is worthwhile.

  • Target: copy the next upgrade and its requirements from the live game. Do not budget for an imagined final build.
  • Materials: list required amount, amount owned, shortfall and a source you have actually unlocked. Mark unknown costs as unknown.
  • Dependencies: include the weapon, partner or unlock needed for the proposed interaction. An unowned partner leaves the plan conditional.
  • Opportunity cost: name the other upgrade using the same resources and the task it would help. Compare solving a survival failure now with funding another damage option later.
  • Trial cost: check the displayed entry cost and attempt limits before repeating content. Prefer an available low-cost practice setting when it tests the same condition.
  • Stop condition: decide what result ends spending, such as clearing the target reliably. If the upgraded run fails for the same reason, diagnose again before the next purchase.

A hypothetical choice between two owned roles

Your damage role A reaches the second boss phase but repeatedly dies there. Owned role B has an effect whose current skill text may help survival. First trial B without a major upgrade and record whether the same attack still ends the run. If survival improves, price only the next useful step for B. If the deaths continue because you miss the same dodge, practise that sequence before spending. A and B are placeholders; no hero performance or resource saving is asserted.

Investment questions

Should I replace a built hero because a new one is available?

Write the unmet job first. If your existing team already completes the target and the new kit has no verified use for your next task, defer the replacement and retain your resources.

Can I turn official skill numbers into a tier list?

Skill descriptions establish stated effects and conditions. A comparative ranking also needs encounter, weapon, unlock, equipment and execution context. This page does not supply that testing.

Where do I go after choosing a candidate?

Use the team building and troubleshooting guide to test the candidate's job in your actual roster. See our editorial policy for how sourced facts and original analysis are distinguished.
