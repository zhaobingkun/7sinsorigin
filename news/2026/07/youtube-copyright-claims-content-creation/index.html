Seven Deadly Sins Origin YouTube Copyright Claims and Creator Policy

Published: July 30, 2026 · Official creator notice

Direct answer: The Seven Deadly Sins: Origin team says it has withdrawn recent YouTube copyright claims affecting creators. It says the claims came from an automated project-monitoring process outside the Origin team's direct review, and future claims will be reviewed by the team before submission.

Does the team claim videos simply for discussing leaks?

The official notice says the Origin team does not report or enforce against a video or channel simply because it discusses leaked information. It does not encourage leak-based content, but says no separate policy or enforcement measure for that discussion content has been established at this time.

Why were the recent claims filed?

After reviewing the cases with YouTube, Netmarble says the claims were automatically submitted through a separate monitoring process for the overall project. The Origin team says it has withdrawn those claims and will clarify the monitoring criteria.

What activity can still face action?

The notice draws a boundary between covering leaked information and directly obtaining or aggressively leaking confidential information. Netmarble says copyright warnings and other action will continue against direct leaking attempts and conduct involving confidential material.

Practical reading for creators

This is an official policy clarification, not permission to redistribute copyrighted assets or confidential materials. Creators should read the original notice and platform rules before publishing, and should avoid presenting unverified leaks as confirmed game information.
